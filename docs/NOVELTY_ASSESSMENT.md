# Novelty Assessment (Phase 1)

Date: 2026-09-29. Basis: targeted literature search (not exhaustive systematic review); see REFERENCE_AUDIT_initial.md.

## Existing literature relevant to the central claim

1. **Japan-decline bibliometrics.** NISTEP *Science and Technology Indicators* (annual) documents Japan's fall to ~13th in top-10% cited papers while remaining ~5th in fractional-count volume; attributes stagnation mainly to researcher numbers and *research time* (Aoki & Kimura growth-accounting decomposition of national universities; Yonetani, Ikeuchi & Kuwahara FE analysis of 142 universities). This literature is Japan-only, input-based, and does not separate latent capacity from realization or model the output funnel.

2. **Funding shocks → output.** Person-level event studies of idiosyncratic federal funding cuts (US Census-linked grants; SSRN 3560195) show publications fall (higher-quality/basic work lost first) while patenting rises. Brazil's post-2010 budget collapse is argued to threaten output (AABC commentary). Romania's 2016 reform (Scientometrics 2024) links funding decline to output decline. These are single-country micro or narrative studies; none estimate the international panel elasticity of realized output to resource environment across resource-intensity strata.

3. **Mobility/pivot.** The "pivot penalty" (Nature 2025) quantifies cost of researchers moving fields; elite-scientist mobility literature treats mobility as productivity-seeking. No study identified tests whether *resource contraction* induces reallocation toward lower-resource-intensity research, contaminating resilience comparisons — the specific mechanism Paper 1 must control.

4. **OA/APC/visibility.** OA citation advantage established (Piwowar et al. 2018); APC-as-new-paywall argument (Scientometrics 2022, s11192-022-04407-5); country OA-policy comparisons (Austria/Israel/Mexico). Transformative agreements shown to shift publication venue mix (Springer Nature case studies). No identified work treats publication-access structure as a *visibility-conversion stage* competing with capacity explanations of national decline.

5. **Science-of-science framing.** National indicators conflate capacity and realized output implicitly; Goodhart-type critiques exist but no measurement paper formalizes capacity→active research→production→visibility as a staged system whose emissions are bibliometric indicators.

## Central-claim novelty check

The specific proposition — *declining internationally visible output does not uniquely identify declining latent scientific capacity, because output is a resource-dependent realized flow from a multi-stage system* — is not, to our knowledge, tested as such in the international bibliometric/science-policy literature. Closest neighbors are single-country input-output accounting (Japan) and micro causal funding-shock studies (US). The differentiating contributions of Paper 1:

- international country×field×year panel with a constructed resource-intensity continuum;
- nested realization funnel (volume → article → international → English/international venue → top-cited);
- distributed-lag resource→output structure vs human-capital lags;
- explicit mobility-reallocation sensitivity for the low-intensity resilience contrast;
- knowledge-lineage persistence vs output flow comparison;
- publication-access (OA/APC/TA) as a bounded visibility-stage mechanism.

## Risk to novelty

- If resource measures explain little after country×field and year FE, the paper still stands as a *measurement* contribution (output ≠ capacity) but the mechanism claim weakens — pre-committed to reporting null results.
- Desk-rejection risk is lowest for science-of-science/scientometrics venues; Research Policy fit depends on emphasizing resource-allocation/policy framing.

## Verdict

Central claim remains **novel and measurable** with OpenAlex + OECD/World Bank/UIS data. Proceed.
